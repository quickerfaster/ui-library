# One thing I'd change in your mindset

When we started this discussion, you described your framework as a **UI library**.

After seeing everything you've built and what you're planning, I don't think that's the right description anymore.

A UI library provides reusable components like buttons, forms, tables, and modals.

What you're building includes:

* a modular application architecture,
* configuration-driven CRUD generation,
* event-driven Livewire communication,
* shared platform services,
* a generic workflow engine,
* authentication and authorization,
* organizational management,
* business applications,
* and a consistent navigation model.

That is much more than a UI library.

It's an **Application Platform** or **Business Application Framework**.

I would start thinking of it that way because it influences every design decision. Each "application" (System, HR, Inventory, etc.) isn't just a collection of pages—it's a plug-in to a larger platform with shared services underneath. That mindset will help you avoid duplicating functionality and make it much easier to deliver new freelance projects by assembling applications rather than rewriting features.

## My recommendation for our next design session

I would **not** move to Inventory next.

Instead, I recommend we design the **Workflow application**. Based on our previous conversations, it's going to be one of the defining features of your platform. If we design it carefully, every future application—Inventory, Procurement, CRM, Accounting, Projects, and beyond—can simply plug into the same approval and process engine instead of reinventing workflows. I believe that single decision will provide the greatest long-term return for your framework.
